Projects

Formula Student race car

SolidWorks, ANSYS, Lotus Shark, Vehicle Dynamics

Suspension, wheel hubs, and uprights, checked with FEA.

Download research paper (PDF)
DetailsHide details

I designed and optimized the suspension of our Formula Student car to improve handling, stability, and overall performance. I also developed the hubs and uprights, calculating the loads on each part and checking it with FEA for stress, deformation, and factor of safety. For Supra 2026, I am the Vehicle Dynamics Lead. I also co-authored a research paper on this work, available to download above. It covers the hub and upright design for a 10-inch wheel, with hubs in Al 7075-T6 and uprights in Al 6061-T6, a reported 15% weight reduction, and a safety factor above 1.5 in every analysis.

Front and rear wheel hub design

SolidWorks

Separate front and rear hubs, designed to be light while staying simple to machine. The splined shaft end and bolt pattern are visible here.

Front hub factor of safety

ANSYS structural analysis

The lowest safety factor on the front hub is about 2.4, in a ring around its centre. Most of the part sits far above that, up to 15, so the hub is safe with room to remove weight.

Front and rear upright design

SolidWorks

The uprights carry the wheel bearings and the suspension and steering connections. They are shaped with pockets to save weight and for easier manufacturing.

Front upright total deformation

ANSYS structural analysis

Under load, the front upright moves by at most about 0.46 mm, at the tip of the steering arm. The body around the bearing bore barely moves, so the wheel stays properly aligned.

Suspension kinematics result

Lotus Shark

The suspension model, with the links, mounting points, and tyres, used to study how the wheels move through travel and steering. This work guided the handling and stability goals.

Powertrain simulation

MATLAB Simulink

Vehicle model with motor, gearbox, and battery. At about 43 km/h it covers 24 km, drawing about 19 kW from the battery, and the charge level falls from 100% to about 60%.

Motor driver simulation

MATLAB Simulink

Speed and torque response of the motor drive. When a 32 Nm load is applied, the torque is adjusted so the speed settles around the 2500 rpm target.

Motor cooling radiator

Flow simulation

Water-cooled radiator for the motor. The fluid cools from about 413 K at the inlet to about 294 K as it passes through the finned tubes.

Chain drive sprocket analysis

ANSYS structural analysis

Stress and deformation of the sprocket in the 3:1 chain drive. Peak stress is about 17 MPa, near the bore, and the deformation stays under 0.004 mm.

Open differential analysis

ANSYS structural analysis

The differential assembly checked under moments from 100 to 1500 N-mm in structural steel. The largest deformation is about 0.09 mm.

Battery module and interconnects

CAD

Cell layout with copper busbars: 36 cells in series, each made of 4 in parallel. That is 144 cells, about 129.6 V and 200 Ah.

Battery liquid cooling

Flow simulation

Coolant flow through the battery cooling channels. The cooling was sized to keep the cells in a 20 to 25 degree C range at peak load.

Patent

Automatic footrest for two-wheelers

Patent application published, Indian Patent Office

Footrests that deploy when a pillion rider sits, with Arduino control.

Download patent publication (PDF)
DetailsHide details

A two-wheeler footrest system that deploys and retracts automatically based on the weight on the pillion seat. I designed the full mechanical architecture, wrote the control logic in C++ on Arduino, and integrated the sensors and actuators with safety in mind. I am one of six named inventors.

Application No. 202521100776, filed on 17 October 2025 and published in the Patent Office Journal on 14 November 2025. The full publication is available to download above.

Where the parts sit

Footrest system

A pressure plate under the pillion seat rests on a load cell. The signal goes through an HX711 amplifier to an ESP8266 NodeMCU, which drives a servo motor to deploy or retract the footrest. The scooter battery powers it, and a killer switch on the handlebar can cut the system.

Control circuit

Footrest system

The load cell feeds the HX711 amplifier, which sends the weight reading to the ESP8266 NodeMCU. The NodeMCU drives the servo, reads the killer switch, and lights an indicator LED.
